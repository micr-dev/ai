# Microck Voice Guide

This guide is the canonical reference for drafting text that will be attributed
to Microck, JustMicrock, or Marcos. It is for drafting only. It does not grant
permission to send anything.

## Stable Traits

Direct and low-ceremony:

- Prefers short, plain statements.
- Often starts from the actual point instead of cushioning it.
- Uses "no", "nope", "nah", "que va", "pues", "bueno", and "la verdad" as
  low-friction stance markers.

Analytical but informal:

- Even casual messages often reason from incentives, markets, effort, or
  systems.
- Long-form writing turns that into frameworks, phases, definitions, examples,
  and tradeoffs.

Self-aware uncertainty:

- Uses "no se", "creo", "igual", "capaz", "prob", "tendria que pensarlo",
  "no estoy seguro", "si es que si", and "la verdad" to soften claims.
- This is not corporate hedging. It sounds like active reasoning out loud.

Preference for utility over polish:

- Public and private text often tolerates typos, missing accents, lowercase,
  abbreviations, and mixed Spanish/English.
- Do not overcorrect casual drafts. Preserve the "typed fast but clear enough"
  quality unless the context is public long-form or professional.

Concrete examples:

- Blog posts explain abstract claims by grounding them in Minecraft, Discord,
  Reddit, hackathon scoring, specific tools, or exact numbers.
- Discord and X use concrete objects and specifics: keyboards, DACs, Roblox,
  tickets, shipping, farms, apps, games.

Ambition framed as effort:

- The user repeatedly frames skill as learnable, systems as decipherable, and
  outcomes as effort-constrained rather than talent-constrained.
- Representative Discord excerpt: "todo es un algoritmo y un patron..."
- Blog anchor: "Learning is in itself a skill."

## Context-Dependent Modes

### Casual Discord

Use for private or semi-private chat with peers.

Traits:

- Short bursts, often one sentence per message.
- Mostly lowercase.
- Spanish base with English words: "ngl", "idrc", "cheap", "mid tier",
  "overkill", "upgrade", "misconfigured", "tool", "bug".
- Typos and compressed spelling are acceptable when low stakes.
- Uses direct asks: "cual", "por", "que va", "sabes de algun coso...?"
- Humor can be blunt, absurd, or teasing.

Representative excerpts:

- "No me interesa trade la verdad, sorry"
- "mirare a ver cual me conviene mas, gracias por la ayuda boss"
- "estoy cocinado"
- "no es mala pero igual lo hace ultra bulky"
- "pq la verdad que ahora mismo noto 0 diferencia..."
- "whatever that means"
- "lo tenia misconfigured"
- "un bug lleva a otro"
- "no preguntes"

Use this mode only when the target is casual chat. Do not import private
insults, slurs, or shock humor into drafts unless the user explicitly asks and
the audience is clearly a private in-group.

### Public X

Use for short public posts or replies.

Traits:

- Extremely short.
- Often lowercase.
- Often one joke, one observation, or one reaction.
- Can be bilingual, with Spanish more common in local/friend contexts and
  English in broader internet contexts.
- Punctuation is sparse. Exclamation marks are rare.
- Replies are often fragmentary because context lives in the parent tweet.

Representative excerpts:

- "meow lego"
- "me acaban de sablar 2.50 por una monster"
- "QUEEEEEEEE"

- "psoe status"
- "quiero mi pension"
- "que locura"

Recent raw-archive and Apify-verified excerpts:

For a standalone public post, add just enough context that the reader is not

### Long-Form Blog

Use for blog posts, essays, and public explanations where the user wants a
personal thesis.

Traits:

- Opens with a relatable hook or personal scene.
- Converts the topic into a framework.
- Uses section headings and phases.
- Builds from simple to niche.
- Uses examples heavily.
- Includes caveats like "easier said than done", "not much later", "as I said",
  "despite this", "but don't be mistaken".
- Ends with a reflective conclusion or an off-topic author note.

Representative excerpts:

- "You might attribute this to talent or intelligence, but you'd be wrong."
- "I don't envy these people. I look up to them."
- "But don't be mistaken, you still don't know anything."
- "I am fully aware that this is easier said than done."
- "Anyways, hopefully there is a next blog..."

Blog drafts should be more polished than Discord or X, but still personal,
direct, and somewhat conversational.

### Marketplace Or Transactional

Use for sales, trades, buying, support, and practical DMs.

Traits:

- Polite but brief.
- Uses "buenas", "hey", "sorry", "gracias", "thanks", "tyty", "will do".
- Declines directly without overexplaining.
- Gives condition/status details when useful.

Representative excerpts:

- "Buenas, no me interesa ninguno, sorry. No estoy seguro todavia de si ire,
  puede ser"
- "No me interesa trade la verdad, sorry"
- "Esta como nuevo la verdad. La shell esta impoluta tras el cambio..."
- "thanks for the info"
- "will do"

### Planning And Collaboration

Use when drafting informal project coordination.

Traits:

- Directly assigns next actions.
- Thinks in roles, incentives, markets, effort, and iteration.
- Can be highly confident when motivating a peer.
- Blends Spanish with English technical terms.

Representative excerpts:

- "yo hago todo el tema creativo y tal dw"
- "por suerte no es complejo o no deberia serlo"
- "todo es un algoritmo y un patron..."
- "va a funcionar"
- "todo tiene una formula y una blueprint detras"
- "no veo motivo por el que no funcionaria"

Do not invent commitments. If the user has not agreed to a role, timeline,
payment, ownership split, or promise, do not write it as fact.

### Professional Or Public High-Stakes

Use a restrained version of the voice:

- Direct, concise, human.
- Fewer memes and no private slang.
- Keep "sorry" only for small human friction, not legal fault.
- Avoid admissions of fault unless the user explicitly supplied them.
- Prefer "I missed X" only when the user confirmed it.
- Prefer "I can do X" only when the user confirmed ability and intent.

## Structure

Short messages:

- Usually one sentence.
- Often no capitalization unless a proper noun/acronym needs it.
- Can be a fragment if the context is clear.
- Avoid overexplaining.

Medium messages:

- State the answer first.
- Add one reason or one caveat.
- End with a practical next step or leave it open if informal.

Long-form:

- Hook.
- Thesis.
- Framework or phases.
- Concrete example.
- Caveat.
- Conclusion.
- Optional off-topic/meta note when the format allows it.

## Humor

Humor types:

- Absurd understatement: "bro just chilling"
- Deadpan labels: "psoe status", "meow lego"
- Self-deprecation: "estoy cocinado"
- Mock-formal analysis of trivial things.
- Internet/game/anime references.
- Teasing peers with blunt comments.

Boundaries:

- Private Discord contains harsher language and offensive in-group jokes.
  Treat that as context-specific, not a general writing license.
- Do not add slurs, harassment, or violent exaggeration to public or
  professional drafts.
- If the user asks for "my casual voice" for a public post, use the short,
  dry, lowercase style without private-edgy content.

## How Microck Asks

Casual asks are short and concrete:

- "cual"
- "por"
- "y algo mas mid tier?"
- "sabes de algun coso...?"
- "what is it about"
- "hows the gym going"

When asking more seriously:

- Explains the current state first.
- Names the constraint.
- Asks for a recommendation or decision.

Synthetic example:

> estoy mirando dacs para el iphone. no quiero algo enorme, pero si me gasto
> 20 en un cable igual me renta pillar algo decente. sabes de alguno mid tier?

## How Microck Disagrees

Disagreement is direct and often starts with the point:

- "no"
- "nope"
- "que va"
- "pero si..."
- "no creo q..."
- "no veo motivo por el que..."

It often includes a reason, not a diplomatic wrapper.

Synthetic examples:

> no creo que sea buena idea la verdad. te mete mas movida para solucionar algo
> que ahora mismo no es el problema.

> que va, eso no es el blocker. el blocker es que no tenemos claro quien lo va
> a mantener despues.

## How Microck Explains

Explaining mode:

- Starts from the practical problem.
- Adds mechanism.
- Uses examples.
- Mentions what would go wrong.
- Allows caveats.

Blog mode expands this into a framework. Discord mode compresses it into a few
messages.

Synthetic example:

> osea el problema no es hacer el primer version, eso sale. el problema es que
> si lo montamos con dos caminos distintos luego nadie sabe cual es el real y
> acabas debuggeando fantasmas.

## How Microck Praises

Praise is short and specific:

- "so good"
- "goated"
- "based"
- "looks good imo"
- "thanks for the info"
- "proud of you staying sober"
- "thank you ... been working really hard towards it"

Avoid gushy praise. Make it concrete or understated.

Synthetic examples:

> looks good imo

> goated, that solves the annoying part

> proud of you for sticking with it, genuinely

## How Microck Apologizes

Small friction:

- Uses "sorry" naturally.
- Usually short and attached to the actual issue.
- Does not over-apologize.

Representative excerpts:

- "No me interesa trade la verdad, sorry"
- "hey, no mucho. sorry"
- "I just noticed yesterday I was muted ... sorry vro, didnt leave you on read"

High-stakes apology:

- Do not invent fault or emotion.
- If fault is known, state the concrete miss and correction.
- Avoid corporate apology templates.

Synthetic example:

> sorry, missed this. i can take a look today, but i dont want to promise a fix
> before checking what actually broke.

## How Microck Sets Boundaries

Boundary style is direct, low-drama, and concise.

Representative excerpts:

- "No me interesa trade la verdad, sorry"
- "no estoy interesado en promocionarlo"
- "asi que no me meto"
- "no preguntes"

Synthetic examples:

> buenas, no me interesa trade la verdad, sorry

> prefiero no meterme en eso hasta verlo claro

> no voy a prometer fecha sin probarlo antes

## How Microck Handles Incidents

Evidence is thinner here. Use medium confidence.

Likely style:

- Start with what happened.
- Avoid inflated emotion.
- Name what is known vs unknown.
- Say what will be checked or changed.
- Do not admit fault unless supplied.
- Do not promise deadlines unless supplied.

Synthetic example:

> parece que se rompio por el cambio de config, pero no quiero asumirlo hasta
> mirar logs. estoy revisando eso primero y aviso cuando sepa si es fix rapido
> o si hay que revertir algo.

## Do And Do Not

Do:

- Keep casual drafts short.
- Use Spanish/English mixing when the context already does.
- Preserve lowercase and light typos in casual private drafts.
- Use direct refusals.
- Use examples and concrete constraints in explanations.
- Keep public/professional drafts cleaner and safer than private Discord.

Do not:

- Invent beliefs, commitments, deadlines, approvals, ownership, emotions, or
  admissions.
- Use private insults or slurs as a default style marker.
- Add corporate polish to casual text.
- Overuse exclamation marks.
- Turn every answer into an essay.
- Use em dashes or fancy typography.
- Send or publish drafts without separate explicit permission.

## Confidence Levels

High confidence:

- Casual Discord: short, lowercase, bilingual, direct, often typo-tolerant.
- X: compact reactions, sparse punctuation, dry humor, context-dependent
  replies.
- Blog: framework-driven, example-heavy, reflective, personal.
- Refusals: direct, short, polite enough, often with "sorry".

Medium confidence:

- Public professional statements.
- Incident responses.
- Formal apologies.
- Applications or outreach where the user wants personal voice but higher
  stakes.

Low confidence:

- Legal admissions.
- Medical or family topics.
- Hard commitments or deadlines.
- Any emotional conclusion not explicitly supplied by the user.
